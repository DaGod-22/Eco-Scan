The Science of Waste

Why does it matter which bin you choose? The answer is all about chemistry, energy and biology.

Decomposition: Two Very Different Fates

When organic waste breaks down, the result depends entirely on whether oxygen is present.

In Landfill — No Oxygen

Anaerobic decomposition

Buried deep and squashed, food scraps rot without oxygen. Microbes that thrive without air produce methane (CH₄) — a greenhouse gas about 28× more powerful at trapping heat than carbon dioxide.

Methane leaking from landfill is a major driver of climate change.

In the Green Bin — With Oxygen

Aerobic composting

In South Australia's green bins, organics are turned and aerated in large-scale composting. With oxygen, friendly microbes break waste down into rich, dark compost — a natural fertiliser — instead of methane.

Same banana peel. Totally different outcome. That's why sorting matters.

About This Project

Everything you need to know about how EcoScan SA was researched, built and improved.

Introduction

I came up with the idea for EcoScan SA because I noticed how often my friends and family stand in front of the school or kitchen bins completely confused about where to throw away their rubbish. Even though South Australia is really good at recycling, a lot of people still make mistakes. They accidently put things like soft chip packets into the yellow bin, which jams up the sorting machines at our local recycling centres.

My project is a mobile website that fixes this problem by making waste sorting easy and fun. I designed an interactive "EcoScan" camera scanner where you can see exactly which household bin an item belongs to according to South Australia's Which Bin rules. I also built a drag-and-drop game to help people practice. The science goal of my site is to teach people why correct sorting matters. For example, when organic food waste goes into the landfill bin, it gets trapped under piles of trash with no air, which creates a dangerous greenhouse gas called methane. But if it goes in the green organics bin, it gets turned into rich compost for SA farms. I want my app to show that small everyday choices can help save energy and protect our environment.
