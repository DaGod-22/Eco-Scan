About This Project

How EcoScan SA was researched and built, what its limits are, and where every fact came from.

Report

I came up with the idea of EcoScan SA because I saw people like my grandparents who come from different countries completely clueless about where to put their rubbish, as there are many bins in Australia. Even people who have been living in Australia for a long time though, like me or my parents, also get confused about where to put the rarer things, such as devices, batteries or magnets. This is why I thought of the idea of EcoScan SA.

My project is designed to assist people in knowing where to put their rubbish. Be it green bins, red bins or special waste disposal, our scanner covers it all. And if you don’t have the image with you, check out our comprehensive database of many waste materials, ranging from greasy pizza boxes to e-waste (such as phones or devices). Aside from our scanner, we also have a fun minigame designed to educate you about waste and where it goes. If you don’t know why, at the end of every round, my website educates you about your mistakes and why you were wrong.

The philosophy of my site is that it isn’t just important to educate people about where they should put their rubbish, but rather to teach them why the correct sorting matters. For example, when organic food waste, such as banana peels, goes to the landfill bin, it gets trapped under piles of trash with no air, which creates a dangerous greenhouse gas called methane (CH4). Methane is 80 to 86 times more damaging than carbon dioxide over its first 20 years in the atmosphere, and still 28 times more damaging over a 100-year period, meaning that it can negatively affect climate change greatly. But, if the food waste goes into the green organics bin, it gets turned into rich compost or fertiliser for farms, like Jeffries and Peats Soil.

AI Disclosure Statement

This project was created with the assistance of Arena.ai. The AI was used to generate the site’s code, layout and interactive features, while I (the student author) directed the logical flow and design and verified the accuracy of the science data and bin-sorting rules against the South Australian sources listed below.

The object detection runs in the browser using Hugging Face Transformers.js and the Xenova/detr-resnet-50 model. No image ever leaves the device. The model’s outputs are treated as a starting point, not as an authority: every disposal rule the app states comes from a listed South Australian source, and the scanner is designed to say “uncertain” rather than to guess.

What the scanner can and cannot do

The scanner runs a real object-detection model (Xenova/detr-resnet-50) entirely in your browser. It recognises 90 object classes. That is object recognition, which is not the same thing as waste classification.

  • Object recognition — "this is a bottle." Reported as detection confidence.
  • Material inference — a bottle is probably rigid plastic or glass, but a photo cannot prove which. Each possibility is weighted.
  • Disposal rule — the material and the object together are mapped to a South Australian category.
  • Disposal confidence — detection confidence × material agreement × how much the answer depends on things a photo cannot show.

When disposal confidence is low, the scanner refuses to name a bin and says "AI identification is uncertain" instead. It also refuses when the model detects nothing, when it detects something it has no rule for, or when a scan fails. A crash is never presented as a disposal instruction.

Honest limitation: the model cannot see grease, cleanliness, whether glass is broken, or whether plastic is rigid or soft — and those are exactly the distinctions some SA rules turn on. That is why the manual lookup exists, and why uncertain results always link to it.

How the South Australian rules were checked

Every disposal rule in this app was checked against a South Australian source, in this order of preference:

  1. The state Which Bin SA guide published by Green Industries SA.
  2. Green Industries SA guidance on batteries, e-waste and hazardous waste.
  3. South Australian council waste pages, used where the state guide is silent.

Where guidance genuinely varies between councils — tissues and paper towel are the clearest example — the item carries a visible note saying so rather than pretending there is a single statewide rule.

Change Log

v1.0 Initial build

Prompt: "Create a Smart Waste Sorting Guide for South Australia's Which Bin rules…" — generated the base layout, scanner, science content and game.

v1.2 Scanner reliability

Prompt: "Find everything that doesn't work — the scanner is the central feature — then fix the worst ten." Fixed the model-load dead end, stopped failures from announcing a bin, mapped all 90 detectable labels, made the model load on first scan, moved inference off the main thread, built a real backend fallback ladder, drew detection boxes, and released the camera when the tab is hidden.

v2.0 Accuracy & credibility pass

Prompt: "Make it substantially more accurate, reliable, polished, educational and competition-ready." Rebuilt the scanner as a layered pipeline that separates object recognition from material inference from the disposal decision; introduced a disposal confidence distinct from detection confidence, with an explicit "AI identification is uncertain" state; corrected the South Australian rules for crockery, glassware and cutlery; expanded the manual lookup to 34 sourced items; replaced every unsourced science figure with an attributed one and replaced the stub bibliography with 15 real sources; added What/Where/Why/Science structure to every result, teaching feedback in the game, and a local-only feedback tool.

v2.2 Report & model loading

Replaced the generated introduction with my own report, and moved the AI disclosure statement directly underneath it so the two read as one document while staying separate. Corrected the methane figures to 80–86× over 20 years and 28× over 100 years. Fixed two scanner defects: the recovery ladder was escalating to model files of 80 MB and 159 MB after failing to load the 41 MB one, and detection scores were being multiplied by 100 and then clamped to 1.0, which made every scan look maximally certain. Moved the test suite into tests/ so it ships with the project.
